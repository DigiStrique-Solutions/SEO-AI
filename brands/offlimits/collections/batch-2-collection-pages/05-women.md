## Use product details to narrow the shortlist

Once a few styles catch your eye, open the product page for each one. Look at the closure, upper material, sole and insock, then compare those details with your own routine. Lace-ups allow you to adjust the fit, while the sole and insock descriptions can help you decide what kind of underfoot feel you are looking for.

Keep fit in the decision from the start. Think about the socks you normally wear, how much toe room you prefer and whether you want a secure heel for an active session. Check the live size selector immediately before adding a pair to your bag, because available sizes and colours can change.

It also helps to separate a training need from an everyday-styling need. A running-inspired shoe may suit the movement you have planned, while a sneaker may be the easier choice for a casual rotation. If one pair needs to cover more than one setting, read the product details closely and choose the option you can realistically see yourself wearing most often.

## Compare a few pairs before you decide

The fastest way to browse a large collection is to filter first and compare second. Start with the category and size that suit you, then shortlist two or three pairs. Look past the colour and image to the product information that will affect the way you wear the shoe.

Ask a few practical questions. Will you use the pair mostly for workouts, a daily walk or casual plans? Do you want an adjustable lace-up closure? Does the available size match the fit you normally choose? Can you picture it with the clothing you already own? These questions keep the choice grounded in what you will actually use.

Give a new pair an easy first outing when it arrives. Wear it around home or on a short familiar route before making it part of a longer day. This is a simple way to check that the fit feels secure and that the pair has a clear place in your rotation.
