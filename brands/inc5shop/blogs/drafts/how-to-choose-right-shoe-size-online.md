# How to Choose the Right Shoe Size When Shopping Online

The risky moment in online shoe shopping comes just after you find the right pair. The colour is right, the style works, and your usual size is sitting in the selector. It is very easy to click.

Pause for five minutes. A quick foot measurement is more reliable than the number on an old shoe box, especially when you are ordering heels for a wedding or flats for an office week. Measure both feet in centimetres, keep the longer result and match it to the current Inc.5 chart. That small routine is the heart of this guide.

## Before the ruler comes out

You need paper, a pencil and a ruler or tape marked in centimetres. Use a hard floor beside a wall; carpet makes the paper shift. Keep the socks nearby if the new shoes will be worn with them.

Timing helps too. Measure after you have been on your feet for a while rather than first thing in the morning. Stand normally during the process so the result reflects the foot carrying your weight.

## How to measure foot size in cm

Place the paper with one edge touching the wall. Stand on it, heel lightly against the wall, toes pointing forward. If possible, ask someone else to make the mark; bending over can move the foot.

Mark the end of the longest toe. It is not always the big toe. Now measure a straight line from the wall-side edge of the paper to that mark and write the result in centimetres.

Then swap feet. A difference between the two is not unusual, and the longer reading is the safer one to take to the chart. Measure it once more before putting the ruler away. A second pass catches a slanted line or a heel that slipped forward.

That is all “how to measure shoe size” really means at this stage. You are measuring the foot first. The shoe-size label comes after you compare that length with the chart belonging to the brand you plan to buy.

## What if your measurement falls between two rows?

A reading of 24.3 cm does not sit neatly on the women's chart. Width can complicate the choice as well. This is the point to open the exact product page, not a general conversion result from search.

Current Inc.5 product pages say the shoes follow the displayed size chart and advise shoppers with wider feet to consider a larger size. Treat that as product-page guidance, then look at the toe shape and fastening before deciding. If the measurement still leaves you unsure, confirm the size with Inc.5 customer support rather than guessing.

Foot size and shoe size are related, but they are not identical terms. Foot size is the centimetre reading from heel to longest toe. Shoe size is the label you reach after matching that reading to the Inc.5 chart and checking the design you want.

## Size 38 is a number, not a shape

A pointed pump, square-toe mule, T-strap sandal and lace-up sneaker can all carry the same size label and still sit differently around the foot.

Look at the front first. A pointed toe narrows differently from a round or square one. Next, check how the shoe stays on: buckle, back strap, laces or a slip-on opening. Closed shoes also cover more of the sides and heel than sandals or mules. These details do not change the chart, but they do help you judge whether the listed size suits the way you plan to wear the pair.

Sneakers and boots deserve one extra check. Measure in the socks you expect to use with them. A barefoot reading is less useful if the finished outfit includes a thicker pair.

## Four mistakes worth avoiding

**Measuring another shoe:** An outsole or insole belongs to that design. Measure your foot instead.

**Stopping after one foot:** Keep the longer reading. It gives both feet a better starting point.

**Mixing size systems:** Begin with centimetres, find the correct Inc.5 row, and only then read the EU, US or UK label.

**Ordering from memory:** Recheck the current chart and the size note on the pair in your bag. An old purchase is useful context, not a measurement.
