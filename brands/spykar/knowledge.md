# Spykar — brand knowledge

## Positioning

Spykar presents itself as an Indian homegrown, denim-led youth fashion brand. The core public-site idea is **“Fit For Every You”**: variety in denim fits, personal expression, movement and everyday comfort. The About Us page describes the brand as established in 1992 and part of the Bagri family-promoted Metdist Group since 2014.

## Product and channel facts

- Core navigation: men, women, underjeans and accessories.
- Denim is the authority category; shirts, T-shirts, trousers, jackets, dresses, innerwear/loungewear and accessories extend the range.
- The About Us page states 2,250 points of sale, including 300+ exclusive stores, large-format retail and major ecommerce platforms.
- The site is a Shopify storefront with product purchase as the primary conversion and store discovery as an important secondary path.

## Audience

Public copy repeatedly addresses young, self-assured and expressive Indian shoppers. It frames products around real settings—daily wear, coffee runs, travel, brunches, evenings and spontaneous plans—rather than formal fashion expertise.

## Voice and messaging

For blog work, `style-guide.md` is the authoritative source and overrides generic content-writing defaults.

Use:

- Young-at-heart, bold, optimistic and direct language.
- Identity and fit language: “every you,” personal style, mood, movement and self-expression.
- Concrete proof: fit range, fabric, stretch, stitching, durability, breathable construction and styling use cases.
- Short campaign phrases paired with practical product detail.
- India-rooted ideas when the collection or source supports them.
- Indian English spelling, present tense, short paragraphs and sentence-case headings.
- Practical styling advice grounded in colours, fits, textures, seasons and real-life use.

Avoid:

- Unqualified “best,” “top” or sustainability claims.
- Generic luxury language or over-polished corporate prose.
- Invented customer demographics, material certifications or environmental outcomes.
- Treating temporary sale messaging as evergreen positioning.
- Bolded keywords, keyword stuffing, dense text blocks, aggressive selling or vague statements such as “this outfit looks stylish.”

## Blog content rules

- Use a keyword-led title, an 80–120 word introduction, five to eight thematic sections, a practical styling section in most posts, subtle brand integration and an 80–120 word conclusion with a soft CTA.
- Place the primary keyword in the title, introduction and at least one heading. Distribute secondary terms naturally; do not bold keywords.
- FAQs are optional but recommended where useful. Keep answers short, direct, conversational and integrated into the article or placed at the end.
- Target meta titles at 50–60 characters and meta descriptions at 140–160 characters.
- Use a digital-first, mobile-friendly layout with clear section breaks, short paragraphs and sparse bullet lists.
- Final review must check structure, voice, natural keyword use, clarity, flow, introduction, conclusion and publish-ready formatting.
- Every blog package must include a unique, topic-led 4:3 banner, delivered as a master PNG and an optimised 800 x 600 JPG. Follow the creative-direction, file and visual-QA requirements in `style-guide.md`; a blog is incomplete if its banner or QA record is missing.

## Brand values supported by the site

- Precision and craftsmanship.
- Youthful adventure, boldness and self-expression.
- Comfort, fit and freedom of movement.
- Durability and longer-lasting denim.
- Reducing environmental impact through environmentally friendly practices and durable products; stronger claims need additional substantiation.

## Current campaign signal

The Spring/Summer 2026 “Roots of Now” collection blends Indian cultural and natural inspiration with contemporary silhouettes. Its content emphasizes heritage plus hustle, earthy and sunlit colors, breathable materials, relaxed shapes, individualism and movement.

## Competitor frame

Current research frame: Killer Jeans, Levi’s India and Pepe Jeans India. These are evidence-backed working competitors because they overlap on denim, casualwear, youth/fashion demand and the Indian retail market. The brand should confirm its approved commercial competitor set.
