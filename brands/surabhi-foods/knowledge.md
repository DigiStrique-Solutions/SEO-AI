## Positioning

Surabhi Foods presents itself as an Indian condiment brand focused on flavour, purity, convenience, and everyday culinary use. The live storefront sells ketchups, mayonnaise, Jain no-onion/no-garlic products, chutneys and pastes, pizza and pasta sauces, fruit jams, and ginger-garlic paste.

Core brand line observed on the homepage: **“Dil Se Pure.”**

## Business and conversion model

- Shopify direct-to-consumer ecommerce store.
- Offline distribution network is claimed to serve households across India.
- Product purchase is the primary conversion.
- Supporting conversions include Zepto orders, email subscriptions, WhatsApp/contact enquiries, and recipe/blog readership.

## Audience

- Indian household grocery buyers and home cooks.
- People looking for convenient sauces, pastes, condiments, and spreads.
- Jain and other consumers specifically seeking no-onion/no-garlic options.
- Culinary enthusiasts looking for recipes and new uses for familiar condiments.

These are site-derived audience signals, not analytics-validated segments.

## Product and proof points

- Pure vegetarian range.
- Premium, hand-picked ingredients.
- No added colours or flavour enhancers.
- FSSC 22000 certification is claimed on the About page.
- Products undergo rigorous quality checks.
- Jain range is explicitly framed as no onion and no garlic.
- Product pages sometimes use concrete formulation facts, such as tomato-puree percentage or fat content.

Do not strengthen or broaden these claims without product-level evidence. Do not use certification claims in high-stakes copy until current documentary proof and scope are supplied.

## Writing voice

Current site copy is warm, enthusiastic, sensory, and benefit-led. It commonly:

- opens with verbs such as “indulge,” “savor,” “discover,” or “elevate”;
- starts from a dish, cooking moment, or flavour experience;
- moves from sensory description to convenience or product detail;
- uses “we/our” for brand commitments and “you/your” for outcomes;
- uses British/Indian spelling such as “flavour” and “colours”;
- ends with a clear invitation to shop, try, discover, or read.

### Do

- Keep the food or cooking occasion central.
- Use concrete product facts where the source supports them.
- Name the relevant Surabhi product naturally.
- Prefer specific sensory language over generic superlatives.
- Use approachable English suited to Indian home cooks.
- Keep calls to action short and direct.

### Don’t

- Invent health, nutrition, shelf-life, sourcing, certification, or performance claims.
- Call Surabhi the “best,” “leading,” or “number one” without explicit criteria and evidence.
- Resolve the 1989/2006 history contradiction by guessing.
- Name competitors as approved brand facts until the user supplies or approves them.
- Treat AI-detector scores as proof of authorship or quality.
