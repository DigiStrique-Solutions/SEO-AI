## Do you actually need boots for an Indian winter?

In Mumbai or Chennai? Probably not for warmth. But warmth is rarely why women buy them. Our own collection page pitches boots as a confidence shoe first and a cosy one second, and that's how most shoppers wear them. Three situations make the case:

- **North India and the hills, December to February.** Ankle boots cover your feet on genuinely cold days and grip uneven roads that open sandals give up on.
- **Wedding season.** Weddings run from November well into February. A heeled boot under a dress or a lehenga is steadier through a five-hour function than a stiletto sandal, and warmer.
- **Everyday layering.** Slip boots under jeans and a sweater and the whole outfit reads as considered instead of thrown together.

Honest answer: not everyone needs boots for the cold. Almost everyone can use one good pair for the season.

## How to style boots with clothes you already own

A boot only pays off if it works with your existing wardrobe. Here's the map.

**Jeans, for every day.** Tuck skinny or straight jeans into an ankle boot; let a wide leg fall over the shaft. Black or brown, round-toe or Chelsea: that combination repeats endlessly, from a class to a coffee to a Monday at the desk.

**Dresses and skirts, for parties.** A short dress over a block-heel or pointed-toe boot is a strong, warm winter-party look. Cold evening? Add opaque tights.

**Ethnic and Indo-western, for festive days and weddings.** Boots under a kurta, a co-ord set or a flowy anarkali have quietly become a winter-wedding staple up North. Go brown or chikoo to keep the palette warm and of a piece with the outfit; go black when you want it sharp.

**The office.** Keep the heel low and the colour plain. A black Chelsea or a round-toe ankle boot under trousers looks sorted without looking like you tried.

## Looking after boots in Indian conditions

Winter here usually means dust, not snow, with the odd unseasonal drizzle thrown in. Wipe the boots down after a dusty day. If they get damp, let them dry away from a heater or direct sun, not on top of it. Store them standing up so the shaft doesn't fold. Do that much and a good pair sees you through several winters.
