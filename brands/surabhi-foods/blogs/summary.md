## Writing voice learned from the current blog set

### Reliable patterns to preserve

- **Start with a food moment or daily friction.** Stronger practical posts begin with busy mornings, family pizza night, or a familiar assumption such as ketchup belonging only with fries.
- **Make the headline's benefit obvious.** Common shapes are numbered uses, `A vs. B`, `why home cooks...`, product + benefit, and occasion + tips.
- **Move from familiar context to useful detail.** Explain the ingredient or cooking situation, then give applications, mistakes, combinations, storage, or FAQs.
- **Keep Indian home cooking concrete.** Curries, gravies, biryani, paneer, Indo-Chinese dishes, wraps, snacks, and family meal occasions make the advice recognisably relevant.
- **Blend sensory and practical value.** `Flavour`, `tangy`, `rich`, `aroma`, and `golden` sit beside time saving, easier preparation, consistency, and less cleanup.
- **Use reader-facing language.** `You/your` carries advice; `we/our` is reserved for brand commitments and the closing product bridge.
- **Introduce the product after earning relevance.** The best examples establish the cooking need first, mention Surabhi naturally in the solution, and return to a short invitation near the end.
- **Prefer British/Indian spelling.** Use `flavour`, `colours`, `centre`, and `savour` consistently even though source posts sometimes mix forms.

### Structural patterns

- Practical posts use descriptive H2s, short lists, numbered steps or uses, common mistakes, and FAQs.
- Comparison posts define each option, contrast taste/texture/use, then help the reader choose.
- Brand stories use a more emotional arc: commitment or campaign idea, evidence/examples, inclusive promise, and invitation.
- Newer posts are substantially longer and more search-shaped than the short 2023 campaign pieces.

### Quality guardrails

- Preserve the **warm, culinary, approachable intent**, but do not reproduce grammar errors, fragments, repetition, or inconsistent capitalisation from the source.
- Do not turn repeated promotional language into proof. Claims about health benefits, shelf life, local sourcing, carbon reduction, farmer partnerships, facilities, certification, or ingredient quality need current product-level or documentary evidence.
- Preserve the unresolved **1989 vs. 2006** history contradiction. One blog states 1989; the official About page also contains a 2006 inception statement.
- Treat the local-sourcing post's statement about Adinath Agro PFPL as source language, not confirmed corporate structure.
- Avoid generic `best`, `premium`, or leadership claims unless criteria and evidence are supplied.
- Keep calls to action short and specific: `Try it in...`, `Explore the range`, `Shop the product`, or `Read the recipe`.

## Recommended reusable article shape

1. Search-friendly, benefit-led headline.
2. Two-to-four sentence opening grounded in a familiar Indian cooking moment.
3. Plain-language explanation of the ingredient, product, or problem.
4. Scannable use cases, comparisons, steps, or mistakes with concrete dishes.
5. A sourced, product-specific Surabhi bridge.
6. Practical close with one short call to action.
7. FAQs only when they answer genuine selection, use, or storage questions without inventing claims.
