# OFFLIMITS Content Fundamentals

These rules apply to all OFFLIMITS blogs, buying guides and other customer-facing editorial content. Read them with `knowledge.md`; verified product pages and the official technology page remain the source of truth for product claims.

## 1. Begin with the customer's problem

- Open with the specific discomfort, question or purchase decision the reader wants to solve.
- Explain immediately what the article will help the reader choose and how OFFLIMITS can help.
- Avoid generic editorial openings, weather commentary or brand philosophy that delays the answer.
- For seasonal topics, discuss weather only where it changes the product decision.

## 2. Follow the customer's natural buying journey

- Organise guidance around the reader's workout, need or priority before introducing product categories.
- For workout content, use a flow such as cardio/running, strength training, studio or mobility work, and warm-ups/full coverage when relevant.
- Answer practical buying questions: Which outfit suits cardio? What works for strength training? Which shoe suits treadmill sessions? Which model offers softer cushioning, greater ventilation or more grip?
- Assume the reader may be buying one item, not a coordinated head-to-toe outfit.

## 3. Make OFFLIMITS products the heroes

- Lead with the relevant OFFLIMITS product or product range, then explain why it suits the customer's need.
- The desired takeaway is: “OFFLIMITS has the right product for my workout.”
- Product names such as Gladiator, Ethos, Pulse X, Snow and Sonic should remain more memorable than their individual materials.
- Use direct product links where a specific model is recommended and collection links where the recommendation covers a category.

## 4. Use technology as supporting proof

- Wicktech, Flyknit, mesh, memory foam, EVA, PU, KPU and Phylon/TPR support the product story; they are not the story themselves.
- Follow every feature with the customer benefit it provides in that product.
- Example: “Ethos uses a super-soft EVA insock, giving shoppers a lighter cushioning feel.”
- Keep proprietary technologies separate from product materials. Verified OFFLIMITS technology names are recorded in `knowledge.md`.
- Use **Wicktech**, not `Wiktech®`. Add a trademark symbol only when the official source shows one.

## 5. Give decisive, benefit-led comparisons

- Compare named products by the difference a shopper can feel or use: airflow, cushioning feel, structure, grip, coverage or flexibility.
- Turn specifications into decisions. Explain who should consider each model and for which workout.
- Use concise comparison tables when they make several exact product differences easier to scan.
- Give direct fit guidance where needed: **Choose the size that gives you a secure heel and midfoot fit.**
- Avoid repeated caveats about individual fit varying; customers already understand that.

## 6. Keep the tone confident and conversational

- Sound like a knowledgeable retail editor helping someone make a choice, not a product catalogue or legal review.
- Prefer positive guidance over repeated “do not,” “does not,” “cannot,” “does not prove” or “do not assume” phrasing.
- State what a verified feature does and why it matters. Avoid discussing what product pages have not tested or claimed unless safety or compliance requires it.
- Vary sentence and paragraph rhythm. Avoid repeated “Choose X if…” constructions and formulaic transitions.
- Use plain customer language. Replace awkward wording with a clear benefit, such as “Shorts provide greater freedom of movement and airflow during high-energy workouts.”
- Keep the OFFLIMITS voice motivational, inclusive and value-conscious without unsupported hype or superiority claims.

## 7. Stay focused on the article's promise

- Give most of the space to choosing OFFLIMITS apparel and footwear.
- Keep rain, puddles, commuting, garment storage, care advice and general workout habits secondary unless the title specifically promises them.
- Mention gym-to-casual versatility or styling only when it materially helps the purchase decision; do not let lifestyle commentary overtake performance guidance.
- OFFLIMITS is a footwear-led brand, so shoe comparisons should be among the strongest and most useful parts of relevant articles.

## 8. Close the decision properly

- End with a concise conclusion that recaps the main customer takeaway.
- Reinforce how the right OFFLIMITS apparel or footwear can solve the stated problem.
- Do not introduce new product comparisons in the conclusion.
- Place the CTA after the conclusion so the invitation to browse feels earned and natural.
- Link to the most relevant OFFLIMITS product and collection pages rather than using a generic “shop now” instruction.

## 9. Verify before publishing

- Check every product material, technology, intended use and benefit against current OFFLIMITS sources.
- Never invent performance results, prices, reviews, waterproof claims, universal slip-proof claims or superiority claims.
- Preserve the approved canonical brand name and technology spellings in `knowledge.md`.
- Run the content authenticity and AI-pattern gates required by the workspace before marking customer-facing copy publish-ready.

## Recommended OFFLIMITS buying-guide flow

1. Customer problem and the decision the guide will solve.
2. OFFLIMITS range as the solution.
3. Recommendations organised by workout, need or shopping priority.
4. Named products with feature-to-benefit explanations.
5. Clear product comparison or shortlist.
6. Direct fit or buying reminder where useful.
7. Conclusion that recaps the value.
8. Relevant product and collection CTA.
