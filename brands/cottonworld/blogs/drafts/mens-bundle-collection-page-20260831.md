## Start With the Occasion, Not the Outfit

The most useful combination is the one that matches the shape of your day.

### For a Workday That Runs Into the Evening

Choose a shirt and pants pairing that feels comfortable enough for the commute, meetings and whatever happens after work. A regular fit is a straightforward place to start. Keep the colour palette calm, then bring in familiar shoes or a light layer. The result can feel settled without looking overly formal.

### For a Slow Weekend or a Casual Meet-Up

A T-shirt with shorts or easy pants gives you room to move through a less structured day. The live edit includes both regular-fit and slim-fit T-shirt options, so you can choose the silhouette that you tend to wear most. Add a shirt as a layer if the plan changes, but leave the outfit uncomplicated.

### For Travel Days and Changing Plans

When you are out for longer, repeated comfort matters more than a complicated outfit. A shirt-and-pants combination can be a useful starting point because each piece can return to the rest of your wardrobe later. Pick the fit first, then consider the fabric and colour you will be happy to wear again.

## Make the Look Your Own

Use a curated pairing as a starting point, not a uniform.

1. Pick the piece you need most: a shirt, a T-shirt, pants or shorts.
2. Check the fit and fabric details on each product before choosing a size.
3. Keep one piece familiar and let the other add something new to your weekly rotation.

If a complete look feels right, you can follow it as it is. If only one piece catches your attention, it can still earn its place beside the clothes you already rely on. That is often how a wardrobe grows well: gradually, with choices that make real days simpler.
