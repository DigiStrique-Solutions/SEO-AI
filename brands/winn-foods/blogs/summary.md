## How the Current Content Writes

### Title patterns

- Recipe titles are almost always the bare dish name: “Chilli Paneer,” “Schezwan Noodles,” and “Spicy Eggplant.” This is clear and search-aligned, but it leaves preparation style, time, and product differentiation out of the headline.
- Editorial titles use questions, numbered problem/solution formats, and utility promises: “Are Chinese Sauces Healthy?”, “5 Mistakes…,” “What It Is & Why Every Kitchen Needs It.” One title uses explicit brand wordplay: “Winn-ning Hearts.”

### Intros

- Recipes move quickly from the dish name to a compact flavor/result description, usually naming a principal ingredient and a Winn sauce. The appetite vocabulary repeats “spicy,” “flavorful,” “fiery,” “tender,” “crispy,” and “easy.”
- Editorial posts open with a familiar kitchen problem, craving, rhetorical question, or playful hook. Examples recorded by Firecrawl include a sticky/soggy noodle problem and the line of thought that sauce turns an ordinary meal into something more exciting.

### Structure

- Recipes follow a predictable, practical sequence: short dish description → ingredients → instructions → a brief serving suggestion or closing line. Ingredient groupings such as sauce/base/stir-fry appear when useful.
- Editorial pieces use scannable explanatory sections, numbered mistakes or sauce lists, closing recommendations, and—in the newer utility posts—FAQs. This is a stronger search structure than the shorter early brand stories.

### CTA and product integration

- Product integration is direct and recipe-specific: Winn Dark Soya Sauce in fried rice and mushrooms, Schezwan Chattak Chutney in fried rice, and Green Chilli Sauce in stir-fries. This makes the product’s job concrete rather than attaching an unrelated sales block.
- Editorial posts transition from education into category or collection exploration. Several pages also surface the sitewide `FIRST20` promotion; because offers are time-sensitive, future copy must re-check it rather than inherit it from these references.

### Tone and sentence style

- The dominant voice is friendly, energetic, accessible, and convenience-led. Editorial copy is more playful and persuasive; recipe copy is more functional.
- Sentences are generally short to medium length, with direct verbs and uncomplicated vocabulary. Instructions are imperative and sequential. Exclamation marks and rhetorical questions appear selectively in editorial hooks and CTAs.
- Recurring language includes “Indo-Chinese,” “bold flavors,” “restaurant-style,” “quick,” “easy,” “spicy,” “Winn Sauce,” “stir-fry,” and “at home.”

### Strengths

- Strong alignment between recipe intent, exact dish names, and relevant products.
- Easy-to-scan formats that suit home cooks looking for a quick answer.
- Consistent sensory vocabulary and a recognizable Indo-Chinese positioning.
- Editorial content expands beyond recipes into technique, mistakes, sauce selection, and cuisine education.

### Gaps and cautions

- Publication dates are not reliably exposed on the live pages; Shopify sitemap `lastmod` is preserved separately and must not be represented as a publish date.
- Recipe titles and introductions are often generic. Unique technique, time, serving size, difficulty, substitutions, and “why this works” detail could create more differentiation and utility.
- Several recipe pages appear thin beyond ingredients and instructions, with inconsistent serving guidance, FAQs, and internal links.
- Health and clean-label posts make broad claims around MSG, artificial colors, preservatives, “safer” choices, and long-term wellness. These need product-level substantiation and editorial review before being reused; the brand knowledge file explicitly prohibits generalizing such claims.
- Spelling and naming are inconsistent across snippets (`soy`/`soya`, `chilli`/`chili`, and occasional product-name casing). Establish a product naming and Indian-English style sheet.
- Topic coverage leans heavily toward sauces, noodles, stir-fries, fried rice, and Manchurian dishes. The current keyword-cluster file was not available during this phase, so a quantified cluster gap analysis remains pending.
