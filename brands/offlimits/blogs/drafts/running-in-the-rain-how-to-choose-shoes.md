# Running in the Rain: How to Choose Shoes for Wet and Humid Weather

A road can look manageable after a shower and still feel very different once you pick up the pace. Add humid air and warm feet, and the familiar five-kilometre loop becomes a shoe problem as much as a weather problem.

## Begin with the route, not the forecast

Is the route a damp paved loop, a treadmill session during a downpour or a road with patches of standing water? Each answer changes the decision. A secure heel matters when you change pace. Clear tread helps the outsole meet the ground properly. On a muggy day, airflow through the upper also becomes noticeable before the run is over.

Ethos, Gladiator, Pulse X For Her and Sprinter For Her are breathable running and gym shoes. They make sense for light rain, humid training and damp routes. If water is collecting on the road, change the route instead of expecting a training shoe to behave like a rain boot.

## Fit matters before the first wet step

A loose heel quickly becomes irritating when you slow down or turn around an uneven patch. Aim for a heel that stays in place, a midfoot that feels held without being squeezed and enough room to move your toes.

All four models are **lace-up running shoes**. Work the tension from the lower eyelets upward instead of pulling only at the collar. A brief walk or jog will reveal whether the heel lifts.

**Choose the size that gives you a secure heel and midfoot fit.** Wear the socks you normally run in when checking the size, because sock thickness changes the amount of room inside the shoe.

## Before heading outside

Take one last look at the route and the shoes. Tread packed with dirt cannot meet the ground cleanly. Loose laces undermine an otherwise secure fit. A pace that feels ordinary on a dry morning may need to change around a wet corner.

After the run, loosen the laces and leave the shoes to air naturally before wearing them again. If the insock is removable, drying it separately gives moisture another way out.
