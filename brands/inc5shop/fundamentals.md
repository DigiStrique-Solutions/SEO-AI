# Inc.5 Content Fundamentals

These rules apply to Inc.5 blogs, buying guides, category explainers and other customer-facing editorial content. Read them with `knowledge.md`. Current Inc.5 product and collection pages are the source of truth for product names, materials, construction, availability, prices and sale claims.

## 1. Start with the shopper's immediate decision

- Open with the occasion, discomfort or buying question the reader needs to solve.
- State what the guide will help them choose within the first paragraph.
- Ground the problem in Indian life when relevant: monsoon commutes, college days, office wear, weddings, festive events, holidays or everyday city travel.
- Avoid generic fashion history, broad trend commentary or brand philosophy before answering the query.

## 2. Follow the natural purchase sequence

- Move in this order: **need or occasion -> buying criteria -> suitable styles or categories -> verified Inc.5 options -> comparison -> checklist -> CTA**.
- Explain the criteria before presenting products. Typical footwear criteria include comfort, fit, heel height, stability, construction, maintenance and occasion suitability. Bag criteria include capacity, compartments, closure, strap comfort, material and intended use.
- Organise recommendations by the shopper's use case, not by arbitrary catalogue order.
- Keep care or styling advice secondary unless the title specifically promises it.

## 3. Keep women's footwear at the centre

- Treat women's footwear as the lead editorial category: heels, sandals, flats, mules, wedges, platforms, pumps, boots, ethnic and comfort styles.
- Use men's footwear, Privo, handbags, wallets and backpacks as focused secondary tracks rather than diluting a women's-footwear article.
- Cross-category content is useful only when the reader naturally buys both categories, such as college footwear and bags or occasion footwear and handbags.

## 4. Write for Indian occasions and routines

- Prefer locally relevant situations: festive dressing, weddings, Haldi and Mehendi, Navratri, Raksha Bandhan, parties, office commutes, college, monsoon and End of Season Sale.
- Match the footwear or bag to the actual setting and expected duration of wear.
- Use Western styling references only when they answer the query; do not let them replace Indian ethnic, fusion and occasion context.
- Plan seasonal pieces early enough to help the reader shop before the event or weather peak.

## 5. Make Inc.5 categories and products the practical answer

- Introduce the most relevant Inc.5 category as soon as the selection criteria are clear.
- Use category links for broad choices and current product links for exact recommendations.
- For each recommendation, answer three questions: **What is it? Why does it suit this need? Who should choose it?**
- Name the category or product more prominently than generic feature language.
- Do not append Inc.5 as a final paragraph after pages of generic advice.

## 6. Translate features into shopper benefits

- Follow each verified material or construction detail with the benefit it supports in that specific product.
- Examples of useful decision language: a wider heel base for a steadier feel; an adjustable strap for a more controllable fit; multiple compartments for easier organisation; a lighter construction for daily carrying.
- Do not turn inferred features into performance promises.
- Never claim waterproofing, slip-proof performance, arch support, all-day comfort, durability or universal fit unless the current source explicitly supports that claim.

## 7. Compare by use case, not hype

- Compare styles through differences a shopper can act on: heel height, base stability, toe shape, fastening, coverage, capacity, closure, occasion and maintenance.
- Use a concise table when comparing three or more exact choices.
- Explain trade-offs positively and decisively: which option suits a long commute, a wedding event, daily college use or a compact evening carry.
- Avoid unsupported “best,” “must-have,” “monsoon-proof,” “trending” or superiority claims. If the title uses one, define the criteria and show the evidence.

## 8. Use a warm, confident retail-editor voice

- Sound like a knowledgeable Inc.5 store advisor: stylish, practical, friendly and direct.
- Balance contemporary fashion with comfort, craftsmanship and value.
- Prefer concrete guidance over inflated adjectives, repetitive rhetorical questions or generic motivational filler.
- Vary paragraph and sentence rhythm; avoid repeating formulaic constructions such as “whether you are...” and “choose X if...” in every section.
- Use the canonical brand name **Inc.5** in prose. Preserve exact live product names when linking a product.

## 9. Build trust with verified proof

- Use Inc.5's established-1998 legacy, omnichannel presence and customer reviews only when the current brand source supports the exact wording or number.
- Treat “true to size,” comfort and repeat-purchase sentiment as review evidence, not universal product guarantees.
- Keep prices, discounts, stock and “before your size sells out” language tied to the current page because they change frequently.
- Do not invent materials, performance tests, trend data, customer quotes, ratings or specifications.

## 10. Make SEO feel natural

- Use one clear primary query that matches the shopper's intent, supported by close variants and relevant category language.
- Put the primary topic in the title, introduction, at least one useful subheading and the conclusion without forcing repetition.
- Add descriptive internal links to the relevant Inc.5 collection, product and related blog; avoid generic “click here” anchors.
- Answer likely follow-up questions only when they help the purchase decision. Do not add a padded FAQ section merely for length.
- Refresh year-led, seasonal and trend-led titles when their evidence or merchandising window expires.

## 11. Close the decision and earn the CTA

- Recap the two or three selection rules that matter most for the stated need.
- Do not introduce new styles or claims in the conclusion.
- End with one direct CTA to the closest live collection or verified shortlist.
- Keep sale and urgency language factual, current and secondary to the shopper's decision.

## 12. Verify before publishing

- Check every product name, link, material, construction detail, intended use, price, discount and availability against the live Inc.5 source.
- Recheck seasonal claims, review counts, store counts and other changeable proof at publication time.
- Record the sources used in the authenticity log and label missing evidence instead of filling gaps from assumption.
- Run the workspace authenticity and AI-pattern gates before marking customer-facing copy publish-ready.

## Recommended Inc.5 buying-guide flow

1. Shopper problem, occasion or decision.
2. The criteria that determine the right choice.
3. Relevant Inc.5 styles or categories.
4. Verified options mapped to distinct use cases.
5. A concise comparison or shortlist.
6. Fit, care or styling note only where it helps the choice.
7. Quick buying checklist.
8. Conclusion and relevant category CTA.
