# Local SEO Checklist

Updated: 2026-06-29

Use this checklist for businesses with local intent: storefronts, service-area businesses, hybrid businesses, multi-location brands, local service providers, restaurants, healthcare practices, legal offices, home services, hospitality, local retail, and other businesses where customers search by location, proximity, directions, phone, appointment, or "near me" intent.

This checklist focuses on local visibility across Google Search, Google Maps, local organic pages, local result blocks, Apple Maps, Bing, review platforms, vertical directories, and AI answer surfaces. Use it with the page-level and site architecture checklists when auditing the website itself.

2026-specific notes:

- Google says local ranking is mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance is not something an SEO can directly optimize beyond accurate address, service area, and real-world coverage.
- Google says there is no way to request or pay for better local ranking. Do not sell local SEO as a guaranteed map result placement service.
- Google Business Profile completeness, verification, accurate categories, accurate hours, reviews, photos, products, and profile engagement are still core local inputs.
- Google review enforcement is stricter. Do not incentivize reviews, gate review requests, selectively ask only happy customers, pressure customers onsite, require staff review quotas, or request specific review content.
- Apple Business Connect is now part of Apple Business. Apple Maps place data, photos, actions, and showcases should be treated as part of local visibility.
- Bing Places remains a low-friction local surface and can be managed separately from Google. Do not assume Google coverage automatically fixes Bing.
- Citations and directories should be audited for accuracy, eligibility, and business value. Bulk submissions to weak directories are not a strategy by themselves.
- AI local discovery depends on the same underlying signals: accurate entity data, local pages, reviews, third-party corroboration, and crawlable business details.

## Scoring

Use this severity model:

- `critical`: business cannot be found, profile is suspended or unverified, location data is materially wrong, reviews are policy-risky, or customers cannot contact/visit/book.
- `high`: weakens local relevance, local results eligibility, trust, conversion, or multi-location accuracy.
- `medium`: improves local completeness, prominence, entity clarity, or user confidence.
- `low`: polish, freshness, optional platform coverage, or non-blocking optimization.
- `not_applicable`: business model, location type, platform, or vertical does not use this item.

## 1. Scope And Business Model

Evidence sources: `Human/context`, `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `CMS/code`.

- [ ] The audit scope is defined: one location, service area, hybrid business, multi-location brand, franchise, department, practitioner, local landing page set, or full local presence.
- [ ] The business model is classified: storefront, service-area, hybrid, appointment-only, mobile service, professional practitioner, department, franchise, marketplace, hospitality, restaurant, healthcare, legal, home services, local retail, or local SaaS/service.
- [ ] The primary local conversion is known: calls, directions, bookings, walk-ins, form leads, quotes, appointments, orders, reservations, purchases, app installs, or support visits.
- [ ] The target geography is defined with real business coverage, not wish-list cities.
- [ ] The target query types are identified: near me, city/service, neighborhood/service, branded, category, emergency, open now, best, reviews, directions, booking, price, or product availability.
- [ ] The local results block, local organic, maps, and vertical-platform goals are separated.
- [ ] Stakeholders know that distance/proximity cannot be directly optimized like a title tag.

## 2. Eligibility, Ownership, And Policy Risk

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Human/context`, `CMS/code`, `Manual/local SERP`.

- [ ] The business is eligible for a Google Business Profile because it has a customer-facing physical location or travels to customers.
- [ ] The profile is claimed and verified by the real business or approved manager.
- [ ] Profile owners and managers are current, trusted, and role-appropriate.
- [ ] Former agencies, employees, vendors, and unknown accounts do not retain unnecessary access.
- [ ] Duplicate profiles are identified and resolved through merge, removal, or support escalation where appropriate.
- [ ] Suspensions, restrictions, rejected edits, pending verification, and policy warnings are documented.
- [ ] Business name, address, categories, hours, URLs, and phone numbers follow Google representation guidelines.
- [ ] Virtual offices, coworking addresses, lead-gen addresses, mailbox stores, and unstaffed offices are not used as storefronts.
- [ ] Service-area businesses hide residential or non-customer-facing addresses when required.
- [ ] Multi-location, department, practitioner, and franchise profiles follow Google's separate profile rules.
- [ ] Restricted verticals such as healthcare, legal, finance, alcohol, cannabis, weapons, adult, or age-gated businesses have compliance review before profile or review recommendations.

## 3. Core NAP And Contact Accuracy

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Business name matches the real-world name used on signage, website, stationery, and customer-facing materials.
- [ ] Business name does not include keyword stuffing, city stuffing, taglines, phone numbers, URLs, hours, promotional text, or unauthorized legal marks.
- [ ] Address is precise, deliverable, and matches the customer-facing location.
- [ ] Suite, floor, building, kiosk, or department details are accurate where needed.
- [ ] Map pin is placed at the real entrance or customer-relevant location.
- [ ] Phone number connects directly to the location where possible.
- [ ] Local phone numbers are preferred over generic call-center numbers where practical.
- [ ] Call tracking numbers are configured without breaking NAP consistency or customer experience.
- [ ] Website URL points to the best local landing page, not always the homepage.
- [ ] Appointment, menu, order, reservation, product, service, and quote URLs point to relevant working destinations.
- [ ] UTM parameters are used consistently where tracking is needed and do not create indexable duplicate landing pages.
- [ ] Email, chat, messaging, booking, and contact options are accurate and monitored.

## 4. Categories, Attributes, And Business Details

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Manual/local SERP`, `Human/context`, `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`.

- [ ] Primary category is the most specific accurate category that describes what the business is.
- [ ] Secondary categories are used only when they describe real major services or departments.
- [ ] Categories are not used as keyword lists, amenity lists, or competitor/category stuffing.
- [ ] Category choice is benchmarked against legitimate top local competitors.
- [ ] Attributes are reviewed for accessibility, amenities, service options, identity attributes, payments, parking, dining, delivery, pickup, appointments, and other category-specific details.
- [ ] Attributes are factual, current, and supported by the real business.
- [ ] Business description explains services, products, mission, history, and local differentiators without links, promo spam, keyword stuffing, or unsupported claims.
- [ ] Opening date, accessibility details, payment options, languages, and service options are complete where available.
- [ ] Business details match the website, signage, menus, booking platforms, and customer support scripts.

## 5. Hours, Availability, And Temporary States

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Regular customer-facing hours are accurate.
- [ ] Holiday, seasonal, event, emergency, and special hours are set before they matter.
- [ ] Appointment-only businesses use hours according to platform rules and user expectations.
- [ ] More hours are used for relevant services such as drive-through, pickup, delivery, kitchen, happy hour, pharmacy, or department hours.
- [ ] Temporary closures are used only when the business is actually temporarily closed.
- [ ] Permanent closures, relocations, and rebrands follow the correct profile and redirect handling.
- [ ] Hours match the website, phone system, appointment system, menu/order platform, signage, and major directories.
- [ ] "Open now" visibility is not harmed by missing or stale hours.

## 6. Service Area And Territory Accuracy

Use for service-area and hybrid businesses.

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Human/context`, `CMS/code`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `CRM/calls`.

- [ ] Service areas reflect where the business actually serves customers.
- [ ] Service-area settings are based on cities, postal codes, or regions the business can realistically cover.
- [ ] Service areas are not inflated to target cities the business cannot serve.
- [ ] Each separate service location has separate staff and real operational capacity when separate profiles are used.
- [ ] Residential addresses are hidden when the business does not serve customers at that address.
- [ ] Hybrid businesses show a storefront only when customers can visit during stated hours.
- [ ] Website service-area pages align with real coverage, not doorway city swaps.
- [ ] CRM, call, booking, or job data is reviewed to validate actual service demand by area.
- [ ] Out-of-area leads are routed, disqualified, or explained clearly to avoid poor customer experience.

## 7. Profile Content, Services, Products, Menus, And Actions

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Services list reflects the real services customers search for and buy.
- [ ] Service names are clear, specific, and not stuffed with city or keyword variants.
- [ ] Service descriptions are accurate, useful, and aligned with website service pages.
- [ ] Retail products, in-store products, menus, appointment types, booking links, and offers are used where the platform and business type support them.
- [ ] Product, service, menu, and offer details are current for price, availability, eligibility, restrictions, and fulfillment.
- [ ] Posts, updates, events, and offers are used for real announcements, not thin ranking manipulation.
- [ ] Calls to action point to working pages where users can complete the promised action.
- [ ] Messaging or chat is enabled only if the business can respond reliably.
- [ ] Booking, order, appointment, reservation, and quote flows are tested on mobile.
- [ ] Profile content does not include prohibited claims, restricted content, misleading offers, or policy-risky language.

## 8. Photos, Videos, And Visual Trust

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Apple Business`, `Bing Places`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `Playwright`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Profile has current exterior photos that help customers recognize the location.
- [ ] Profile has current interior, team, product, service, menu, room, vehicle, or work-result photos where relevant.
- [ ] Photos represent the real business, not generic stock imagery.
- [ ] Photos are high quality, well lit, and not misleadingly edited.
- [ ] Logo and cover image are correct, current, and consistent with brand identity.
- [ ] User-uploaded photos are reviewed for accuracy, policy issues, outdated details, and poor customer expectations.
- [ ] Inappropriate or irrelevant photos are reported through the platform rather than ignored.
- [ ] Videos are used where they help show the location, service, product, room, experience, or process.
- [ ] Visual assets on Google, Apple, Bing, Yelp, Tripadvisor, and vertical platforms do not contradict each other.

## 9. Reviews And Reputation Compliance

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `CRM/calls`, `Human/context`, `Manual/local SERP`.

- [ ] Review generation process asks for genuine customer feedback without incentives.
- [ ] Review requests are not gated by sentiment or selectively sent only to happy customers.
- [ ] Customers are not pressured to leave reviews onsite or while staff are watching.
- [ ] Staff are not required to solicit a fixed number of reviews.
- [ ] Staff do not ask customers to mention specific names, keywords, services, cities, or wording.
- [ ] Review request links or QR codes are used only in compliant, neutral customer follow-up.
- [ ] Review replies are timely, helpful, professional, and do not reveal private customer information.
- [ ] Negative reviews receive calm, useful responses and a path to resolve the issue.
- [ ] Fake, spam, competitor, abusive, or policy-violating reviews are documented and reported through official channels.
- [ ] Review removals, profile restrictions, and appeals are documented with evidence.
- [ ] Review velocity, rating distribution, recency, topics, and service mentions are monitored as business intelligence, not manipulated.
- [ ] First-party testimonials on the website are authentic, specific, permissioned, and compliant with relevant laws and platform rules.
- [ ] Yelp-specific review requests follow Yelp rules, including Yelp's restriction against directly asking for Yelp reviews.
- [ ] Vertical review platforms are prioritized based on customer behavior and industry relevance.

## 10. Local Landing Pages

Evidence sources: `CMS/code`, `Firecrawl`, `Playwright`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `GBP`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Each location or service-area page has a clear local purpose and target audience.
- [ ] Page title, H1, intro, URL, and body copy accurately reflect the location or service area.
- [ ] NAP details on the page match the relevant profile and real-world business details.
- [ ] Page includes useful local details: services, neighborhoods, landmarks, parking, transit, service radius, staff, photos, reviews, FAQs, directions, and local proof where relevant.
- [ ] The page has a direct call, directions, booking, quote, menu, order, or appointment path.
- [ ] Embedded maps are useful and do not replace crawlable address text.
- [ ] Store locator or location finder pages link to crawlable individual location pages.
- [ ] Local pages are not thin city-name swaps or doorway pages.
- [ ] Multi-location pages use unique details for each location, not one duplicated template with swapped NAP.
- [ ] Service-area pages are created only where there is real demand, real service coverage, and enough unique value.
- [ ] Local pages link to relevant service, product, team, review, case study, financing, menu, booking, and support pages.
- [ ] Local pages are included in sitemaps only when canonical and indexable.
- [ ] Local pages are tested on mobile for calls, directions, booking, forms, map interactions, and sticky CTAs.

## 11. On-Page Local Relevance

Evidence sources: `Firecrawl`, `Playwright`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `GKP`, `Manual/local SERP`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Primary local query theme is mapped to the best page.
- [ ] Page copy uses natural local language users actually search with.
- [ ] City, neighborhood, region, county, and landmark mentions are accurate and useful.
- [ ] Local modifiers are not repeated mechanically.
- [ ] Service and product terminology matches the profile categories and website navigation.
- [ ] Page answers local buying questions: availability, pricing, service area, wait time, insurance, licensing, parking, delivery, emergency support, accessibility, and appointment options where relevant.
- [ ] Local proof is visible: photos, projects, case studies, reviews, licenses, awards, community involvement, local media, or staff credentials.
- [ ] Local FAQs answer real customer questions and are not added only for schema.
- [ ] Local content avoids unsupported "best in city" claims unless evidence is visible.
- [ ] Internal anchors to local pages are descriptive and natural.

## 12. LocalBusiness Structured Data And Entity Clarity

Evidence sources: `Firecrawl`, `Playwright`, `CMS/code`, `OSS`, `GSC`, `GBP`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] LocalBusiness or a more specific subtype is used where it accurately matches the visible business.
- [ ] Structured data matches visible page content and does not invent hidden business details.
- [ ] Name, address, phone, URL, hours, geo, sameAs, department, menu, price range, payment, service area, and image fields are accurate where used.
- [ ] Multi-location sites use location-specific structured data on location pages.
- [ ] Departments and practitioners are marked up only when they are real public-facing entities.
- [ ] Reviews or ratings markup follows Google structured data policy and is not self-serving or fake.
- [ ] Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList, Product, Service, Menu, Event, FAQPage, or VideoObject schema is added only when the visible content qualifies.
- [ ] Entity IDs, sameAs links, and social/profile links point to official business profiles.
- [ ] Schema is validated with Google's Rich Results Test for supported types and Schema.org validator for broader vocabulary.
- [ ] Browser rendering is checked before claiming schema is absent.

## 13. Citations, Directories, And Local Data Ecosystem

Evidence sources: `Manual/local SERP`, `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `GBP`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Priority listings are claimed or corrected on platforms that matter for the business type and market.
- [ ] Google, Bing, Apple, Yelp, Facebook, Tripadvisor, BBB, industry directories, chamber pages, local media, and vertical platforms are reviewed where relevant.
- [ ] Name, address, phone, website, hours, categories, photos, and services are materially consistent across important platforms.
- [ ] Duplicate, closed, moved, merged, and old-brand listings are identified and corrected.
- [ ] Data aggregator or listing management tools are used only where they save real work at scale.
- [ ] Weak, irrelevant, spammy, or paid directories are not treated as core SEO work.
- [ ] Vertical directories are prioritized by customer trust and actual SERP visibility.
- [ ] Citations point to the correct local landing page when the platform allows it.
- [ ] Listings include real differentiators, services, photos, and booking/contact options where supported.
- [ ] Changes to location data have an owner, source of truth, and propagation check.

## 14. Local Links, Mentions, And Prominence

Evidence sources: `GSC`, `Manual/local SERP`, `Firecrawl`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Local backlinks and mentions are reviewed for relevance, trust, and business value.
- [ ] Real local prominence sources are identified: local news, sponsorships, chambers, associations, schools, charities, events, suppliers, partners, neighborhood groups, and industry publications.
- [ ] Existing unlinked brand mentions are reviewed for possible link opportunities where appropriate.
- [ ] Local PR and sponsorships are real-world activities first, not link schemes.
- [ ] Paid, sponsored, affiliate, or UGC links use appropriate `rel` attributes.
- [ ] Guest posts, local directories, scholarships, badges, and reciprocal links are not used manipulatively.
- [ ] Local anchor text is natural and not exact-match city/service stuffing.
- [ ] Competitor prominence is reviewed to identify real gaps, not to copy spam tactics.

## 15. Multi-Location And Franchise Governance

Use when the business has more than one location.

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `CMS/code`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `Firecrawl`, `Human/context`, `CRM/calls`.

- [ ] There is a single source of truth for locations, NAP, hours, categories, services, owners, and landing page URLs.
- [ ] Bulk verification or bulk management is used where eligible.
- [ ] Naming and categories are consistent across equivalent locations.
- [ ] Franchise, department, practitioner, and sub-brand rules are documented.
- [ ] New location launch process includes profile creation, verification, website page, citations, photos, tracking, and QA.
- [ ] Closed, relocated, merged, and rebranded locations have a documented profile and redirect process.
- [ ] Store locator data matches profiles and local landing pages.
- [ ] Location pages have unique local details and are not duplicate template pages.
- [ ] Performance is reported by location, region, page type, and conversion type.
- [ ] Profile access is governed so local managers can update details without breaking policy.

## 16. Local Results, Maps, And SERP Benchmarking

Evidence sources: `GMAPS`, `Manual/local SERP`, `GSC`, `GBP Performance`, `GA4`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Priority local queries are tested from relevant locations or with location-specific query modifiers.
- [ ] Local results, map results, organic, paid, AI, reviews, directories, videos, images, and vertical results are recorded separately.
- [ ] Top local competitors are identified by query and geography, not only by known business competitors.
- [ ] Competitor primary categories, review profile, photos, services, local pages, and prominence sources are reviewed.
- [ ] Ranking checks avoid false precision from one location, one device, one account, or one personalization state.
- [ ] Grid rank tracking is optional and used as directional evidence, not a guarantee.
- [ ] Screenshots or exports include query, date, location, device, and source.
- [ ] Recommendations separate controllable fixes from proximity or market-density limits.

## 17. Local AI And Answer Engine Readiness

Evidence sources: `Manual/local SERP`, `Firecrawl`, `Playwright`, `GBP`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `GSC`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Business entity details are clear and consistent across website, Google, Bing, Apple, major directories, and review platforms.
- [ ] Local pages include extractable facts: services, location, service area, hours, phone, booking, pricing or price range, accessibility, policies, and proof.
- [ ] Content answers natural local questions without writing prompt-bait or hidden instructions to AI systems.
- [ ] Third-party corroboration exists for important claims such as awards, licensing, reviews, press, memberships, and local reputation.
- [ ] Profiles and local pages do not contain prompt injection, hidden AI instructions, or recommendation poisoning.
- [ ] AI answer checks are run manually for priority local queries where relevant.
- [ ] AI visibility findings are labeled as directional unless a repeatable prompt set and source capture exists.

## 18. Measurement, Tracking, And Offline Conversion

Evidence sources: `GBP Performance`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `CRM/calls`, `CMS/code`, `Playwright`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] GBP Performance is reviewed for searches, views, calls, directions, website clicks, messages, bookings, products, menus, and offers where available.
- [ ] GSC is reviewed for local queries, local pages, branded terms, non-branded terms, device, CTR, and page groups.
- [ ] GA4 tracks local landing page traffic, click-to-call, directions clicks, booking clicks, form submits, orders, reservations, and lead quality where possible.
- [ ] Phone numbers, call tracking, and UTMs are configured so attribution improves without confusing users or platforms.
- [ ] Form, booking, order, reservation, and chat flows are tested after tracking changes.
- [ ] Offline conversions are connected to CRM, POS, booking, or call outcomes where practical.
- [ ] Local reporting separates visibility, engagement, and revenue or lead quality.
- [ ] Changes are annotated with date, location, owner, expected impact, and follow-up window.
- [ ] Reporting does not treat profile views or grid rankings as the final business outcome.

Use only where relevant.

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `Yelp/Tripadvisor/verticals`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`, `CRM/calls`, `Manual/local SERP`.

- [ ] Restaurants verify menus, reservations, ordering links, delivery partners, cuisine, price range, photos, accessibility, popular times, and holiday hours.
- [ ] Hotels verify amenities, booking links, price comparison links, room photos, policies, reviews, and third-party travel platforms.
- [ ] Healthcare practices verify practitioner profiles, departments, insurance, appointment links, credentials, accessibility, privacy, and YMYL content review.
- [ ] Legal and financial services verify practitioner eligibility, licensing, service pages, disclaimers, reviews, and lead intake compliance.
- [ ] Home services verify service areas, emergency availability, licensing, insurance, estimates, before/after photos, and review compliance.
- [ ] Local retail verifies product availability, in-store products, returns, pickup, delivery, accessibility, parking, and merchant feeds where applicable.
- [ ] Automotive verifies departments, sales/service hours, inventory, appointment links, brands, parts, and review routing.
- [ ] Education, classes, churches, venues, and events verify whether regular hours should be used and how schedules are shown.

## 20. Common Anti-Patterns

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `Manual/local SERP`, `CMS/code`, `Human/context`.

- [ ] Keyword-stuffed business names.
- [ ] Fake locations, virtual offices, mailbox addresses, or unstaffed coworking spaces.
- [ ] One service-area profile used to pretend the business has many physical locations.
- [ ] Many thin city pages with only the city name changed.
- [ ] Duplicate profiles for the same business, same practitioner, or same department.
- [ ] Categories used as keyword lists.
- [ ] Outdated hours, holiday hours, closed locations, phone numbers, or appointment links.
- [ ] Review incentives, review gating, onsite pressure, staff quotas, or requested review wording.
- [ ] Stock photos used as proof of real local work.
- [ ] Local landing pages that hide the phone number, address, directions, booking, or service area.
- [ ] Untracked call, direction, booking, and form actions.
- [ ] Bulk citation campaigns on irrelevant directories.
- [ ] Promising map result rankings without distance, competition, and policy caveats.
- [ ] Treating AI local visibility as separate from accurate local entity data and real-world reputation.

## 21. Minimal Audit Flow

Evidence sources: `GBP`, `GMAPS`, `GBP Performance`, `GSC`, `GA4`, `Firecrawl`, `Playwright`, `CMS/code`, `Manual/local SERP`, plus `Bing Places`, `Apple Business`, and vertical sources when relevant.

Use this fast path when the SEO agent needs to audit local SEO.

1. Identify business model, locations, service areas, priority conversions, and priority queries.
2. Verify profile ownership, verification status, policy risks, duplicates, and suspensions.
3. Check GBP core data: name, address, phone, website, categories, attributes, hours, services, products, photos, reviews, and actions.
4. Check local landing pages for NAP, intent, unique local value, CTAs, schema, mobile usability, and indexability.
5. Compare Google, Bing, Apple, Yelp, Tripadvisor, vertical platforms, and major citations for material inconsistencies.
6. Review review process, review quality, replies, policy compliance, and reputation risks.
7. Benchmark local SERPs and real competitors by query and geography.
8. Review GBP Performance, GSC, GA4, calls, bookings, forms, directions, and offline conversion data.
9. Produce prioritized fixes with evidence, owner, expected impact, and confidence.
